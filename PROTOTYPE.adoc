= ⚠️ Prototype status, disclaimers & how this fits the ethics work

[.lead]
*action-trust-layers is a design-stage research prototype (v0), not a
product.* It floats one idea — borrowing DANE/TLSA's layered pinning model
for CI supply-chain trust — and shares it openly for critique.

== What "prototype" means here

* *Design / v0.* This is closer to a proposal than a tool. The model,
  `atl.toml` schema, CLI and evaluation semantics are all provisional and
  may change or be dropped. See link:DESIGN.adoc[DESIGN.adoc] for the
  intended architecture and roadmap.
* *Unproven.* The trust model has not been formally analysed or
  independently reviewed. Treat every claim as a hypothesis to be tested.
* *No warranty.* Provided "as is", with no warranty of any kind, to the
  extent permitted by the licence. See `LICENSE` / `LICENSES`.

== Trust disclaimer (please read)

This project is *about deciding what to trust*, which makes over-trusting it
particularly tempting. Please don't.

* *Not a substitute for SHA-pinning yet.* Full recursive commit-SHA pinning
  is the boring, bulletproof baseline. Until this model is implemented,
  reviewed and battle-tested, do not weaken that baseline on its say-so.
* *A trust policy can be wrong.* "Anchor" and "provenance" layers admit
  actions on softer evidence than an exact hash. That is the whole point —
  and also exactly where a mistake becomes a supply-chain foothold. Any real
  deployment needs its own threat model and review.
* *Security-relevant, not security-audited.* Nothing here has had a security
  audit. Do not place it on a critical trust path in production.

== Interest and collaboration warmly welcome

This is published to *invite scrutiny of the idea*, not to ship a tool.

* Open an *issue* or *discussion* with attacks, edge cases, "this degrades
  unsafely when…", or prior art we should know about.
* Critique of the *ethics/trust framing* (below) is especially valued.
* See `MAINTAINERS.adoc`, `GOVERNANCE.adoc` and `SECURITY.md` before
  contributing.

== Where the ethics thinking lives

Supply-chain trust is an *ethics* question as much as a security one — who
gets the benefit of the doubt, and who carries the risk when it is misplaced.
That reasoning is developed in dedicated sibling projects across the estate:

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Project | Role in the ethics picture

| https://github.com/hyperpolymath/phronesis[*Phronesis*]
| A "practical wisdom" language — the estate's substrate for expressing
  normative reasoning, including judgements about trust and risk.

| https://github.com/hyperpolymath/phronesiser[*Phronesiser*]
| Adds *normative ethical constraints* to AI agents via Phronesis — the home
  for the *values* behind an allow/deny call.

| https://github.com/hyperpolymath/vexometer[*Vexometer*]
| The *interaction-ethics / UX* side: an "Irritation Surface Analyser" for
  the human cost of tools. A trust policy that forces brittle, humiliating
  workarounds is a real cost, and this is how it gets named.

| https://github.com/hyperpolymath/conative-gating[*conative-gating*]
| Sibling prototype applying the same "don't grant blanket permission"
  instinct to *model actions* rather than CI actions.

| https://github.com/hyperpolymath/palimpsest-license[*Palimpsest License*]
| The ethical-use licence family this repo ships under; its Exhibit A sets
  out shared ethical-use expectations for the code.
|===

For the ethics rationale specifically, start with *Phronesiser* (the
*normative* side) and *Vexometer* (the *human-experience* side).
